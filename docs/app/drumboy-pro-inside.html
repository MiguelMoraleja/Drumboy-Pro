A patch, not a chain

Firmware V1.05+ replaces the fixed chain with a small patch. Every source (10 layers, the metronome, line-in) picks where it enters, and every module picks where its output goes: a later module or straight to MASTER. Modules always run in the same order (EQ, Filter 1, Filter 2, Effect 1, Effect 2, Reverb), so a patch can never loop. The default patch is the old serial chain.

On the device: a layer's OUT is on its third instrument page (hold I and press the layer pad, then tap I twice), module outputs and the metronome are on the ROUTING page (press MIXER twice), line-in is the mixer's MIXER IN.

The ROUTING page on the device

Top right lists one line per entry point: the sources that enter there and the chain they travel (L = layer, MT = metronome, LI = line-in, OUT = master). A module that nothing feeds has a grey header.

Diagram (V1.14): while the ROUTING page is open, the lanes give way to the patch as a picture. The six modules and the master are boxes along the top, joined as patched: a route to the next module is a line between the boxes, a longer one goes over them. Modules that get no signal are dimmed. Below, one row per source (ten layers, metronome, line-in) with a mark under the module it enters. A picture is in Screen ideas.

Filter curve (V1.14): while a FILTER page is open, the lanes give way to that filter's response from 20 Hz to 20 kHz, drawn from the same numbers the audio uses. The page's filter is cyan (gray while it is off) with its cutoff as a dotted line; the other filter is gray behind it when it is on. TYPE, SLOPE, FREQ, RESONANCE, DRY and WET all change the curve; DRIVE does not, because it is a gain with a soft clip before the filter.

Slopes (V1.14): SLOPE is now 06, 12 or 24 dB per octave. 24 is new: two filter sections in a row, flat up to the cutoff at the lowest RESONANCE and with one peak as you turn it up. 06 used to be the 12 dB filter mixed half and half with the unfiltered sound, which only ever took 6 dB off; it is now a real gentle filter that keeps falling 6 dB per octave (RESONANCE has no effect on it). A song that used 06 will sound different. 12 is unchanged.

LFOs on the filters (V1.21): on a FILTER page, hold SHIFT and turn FREQ or RESONANCE to pick the LFO (-- none, 01 to 10) that modulates it; the LFO's number shows in yellow under the field. Unlike a layer field, which an LFO changes once per hit, a filter follows its LFO continuously while the sequencer plays: FREQ sweeps up to 3 octaves each way at full LFO depth (an octave is a doubling, kept inside the filter's range), RESONANCE up to half its range each way. The filter is designed again 200 times a second whenever the cutoff moved by even about 0.14 % or the resonance step changed, so the sweep follows the LFO almost continuously; when the sequencer stops, or the LFO is taken off, the set values come back. The LFO's type, rate, phase, invert and depth are set on the LFO page as for the layers. The assignments are saved in the song (an LFD block after the QNT block; older firmware never reads it) and not in the drumkit.

EQ curve (V1.14): while the EQ page is open, the lanes give way to the six bands together as one curve, with a small square for each band at its frequency and gain and its name above (LO, 1 to 4, HI). The curve is gray while the EQ is off.

Volume

What one hit's volume is made of

Every 0–100 setting is cubed

Cubed controls. Level, master, pan and fill velocity all go through the same x³ table, so the top half of each knob does most of the work: 50 is about −18 dB, not −6 dB.

Fill velocities. Fill hits use 100 (high), 75 (the quiet STEP hits) and 40 (the bottom of DESCEND, ASCEND and RANDOM). GHOST is a single hit at 50.

Pitch. See the panel below: from V1.11 pitch is set in semitones.

Soft clip and LIMITER. After master and pan the output is bent smoothly toward a ceiling instead of clipping hard. LIMITER on the mixer page sets that ceiling from −10 to +0 dB (V1.10+); the bend starts at 2/3 of it. At +0 dB, the default, that is 0.667 (−3.5 dBFS), as before V1.10.

Pitch in semitones (V1.11)

On the device: PITCH on instrument page 1 (hold I and press a pad). One detent = one semitone; a fast turn jumps to the next octave line (−24, −12, 0, +12, +24). Holding ADD while turning still picks the pitch LFO.

Speed, not time-stretch. Pitch plays the sample faster or slower: +12 is twice as fast and half as long, −12 half as fast and twice as long. Range 0.25× to 4×.

Older kits and songs keep their exact old pitch, shown the old way (e.g. 1.37), until you turn PITCH; then it snaps to the nearest semitone. Files saved by V1.11 also keep a close old-style value, so older firmware still plays about the right pitch.

LFO on pitch moves up to ±12 semitones at full depth (old-style layers keep the old rule). Above +12 bright samples can sound harsh: the player interpolates linearly, without anti-alias filtering.

Sample preview (V1.11)

On an instrument page, turning SAMPLE plays the selected sample a quarter of a second after the knob stops, straight from the SD card: no loading, no wait, and long samples play in full (loaded samples stop at 5.44 s). The layer pad replays it while the selected sample isn't the loaded one. Nothing changes in the layer until ADD loads it.

PREVIEW (on/off) and PREV LEVEL are on the Metronome page, bottom row. The preview goes straight to the output, before master volume, and stops when you leave the instrument pages, change layer or press ADD.

Euclid

Spreads HITS as evenly as possible over STEPS, then repeats the pattern from the song start to the song end. Same algorithm as the firmware (V1.10).

Quantize (RHYTHM page)

How quantization works (V1.19)

Every hit you record is a position in intervals: 800 to a beat, 3200 to a bar. QUANTIZE (RHYTHM page) is a list of grid points. Try it: pick a grid, move the hit and the strength, and see where the hit lands. The song here is two beats long.

QUANTIZE

On the unit. While the QUANTIZE or the STRENGTH field of the RHYTHM page is selected, the lanes give way to a screen with the same picture: every grid as a row of points over two beats (the chosen one lit: yellow straight, cyan triplet, white where both have a point), what the chosen grid is, and STRENGTH with a hit played a little late and where it lands. It is redrawn as you turn the field; leaving the two fields brings the lanes back.

The nearest point. A hit goes to the nearest point of the grid; exactly halfway goes to the later one. The straight grids (1:64 to 1:1) have a point every 50, 100, 200, 400, 800, 1600 or 3200 intervals. A triplet grid puts three notes in the time of two, so 1:8T has points at 0, 267, 533, 800 ...: a step is not a whole number of intervals, so each point is rounded to the nearest one, but three steps are exactly one beat and nothing drifts.

Mixed grids (1:16+T, 1:8+T) have the points of the straight grid and of its triplet grid (orange and blue above; where both have a point it is black). A hit goes to whichever point is nearer, so one pattern can hold straight and triplet hits. OFF has no grid: every interval is a point, so a hit stays exactly where it was played.

STRENGTH moves the hit only that share of the way to its point: 100 snaps, 50 goes halfway, 0 leaves it where it was played. The distance is rounded to a whole interval, a half away from where you played (3 intervals at 50 % is 2). It counts for hits you record (pads, MIDI IN, the pre-count) from the moment you set it, and is saved with the song.

The end of the song is its start. A hit that would land on the end of the song (or past it) lands on the start instead.

Where it is used. Recording uses the nearest point and STRENGTH. GENERATE BEAT snaps fully. MOVE, SHIFT ALL, INSERT and EUCLID step through the points of the grid (EUCLID only the straight and triplet grids). Changing QUANTIZE moves every existing beat to its nearest point at full strength (OFF leaves them alone); that is why the field is locked until you unlock it. Loading a song leaves each beat where it was saved.

Per-note settings

Each beat can have its own velocity, chance, condition and nudge (the NOTE page). Pick a step, change it, and hear the loops go by. V1.14.

Selected note
One bar of 16ths: click a step to select it (or add a beat). Bar height = velocity; a ring = played this loop, faded = skipped.
The NOTE page on the device

On the device

S
Open it: on a layer's song page tap LAYER SONG (S) twice: song → EUCLID → NOTE → song. NOTE picks the beat (it is highlighted on the timeline).
♪
VELOCITY 1–127 for that beat; until you turn it, the beat plays as FLAT, GHOST or MUTED as before. On a fill it scales every hit. MIDI recording stores each note's velocity here.

CONDITION: A:B plays on loop A of every B (1:4 = first of four), !A:B the other loops, 1ST only the first loop after START or a bank change, PRE if the layer's last conditioned beat played, FKEY only while you hold the F (FILL) key, and !FKEY only when you don't; the screen shows FKEY while it is held. It is not the layer's FILL switch (F + pad).
▮
In the lanes you can see these settings on every layer: a beat's height is its velocity (FLAT full, GHOST two thirds), a hollow beat has a chance below 100 %, and a yellow dot above marks a condition. A layer that does not sound (muted, or another layer is soloed) is dimmed.
↔
NUDGE plays a plain beat up to 12 % of a 16th early or late, without moving it on the grid. Fills keep their timing.
↶
UNDO / REDO: MUTE (the M on the left) + LEFT undoes, MUTE + RIGHT redoes the last beat or bank edit, on any page and while playing: MOVE, SHIFT ALL, insert, CLEAR, fills, paste, whole-bank paste, EUCLID, these NOTE settings and a recording take. ADD

Following a MIDI clock

Set the master's tempo (Loopy Pro often has decimals, because it comes from the length of the first loop) and compare how far the Drumboy drifts. V1.13.

Velocity, in and out
How far behind or ahead of the master (milliseconds)

On the device

⏱
MIDI IN with clock (I/O page): the Drumboy plays only as far as each incoming clock allows and restarts its own timer on it. It can't run ahead, it is at most 1/24 of a beat behind, and nothing adds up over time. Before V1.13 it only copied the tempo (rounded to whole BPM) and then ran on its own.
⟲
No MIDI loops: while it follows a clock it sends no clock and no start/stop of its own on MIDI OUT. Before, a setup that echoed MIDI OUT back to MIDI IN doubled the tempo and ran away to 200 BPM. The sync jack follows the incoming clock.
♪
Notes play at their velocity; chords and fast runs (running status) all play; a note with velocity 0 is silent. Recording keeps soft notes (velocity below 64) as GHOST. MIDI OUT sends each hit's real velocity: FLAT 127, GHOST 64, fills as they play.
▬
CPU and MEM (V1.14): two small bars in the top-right corner of every page. CPU is the audio engine's load: the slowest audio block of the last quarter second as a share of the time a block may take. It rises at once and falls slowly, and turns magenta from 80 %, where dropouts get close. MEM is the sample memory in use: the ten layers' samples as a share of their ten slots (5.44 s each). The numbers beside them are percent.
⏱
Watchdog (V1.14): if the firmware stops responding for 6 seconds (a freeze that is not a crash), the device restarts by itself and shows CRASH 7 S06 P18 L98 (V1.15; before: CRASH 7 AT 0000sssm): S is the stage, what the main loop was doing (1 keys, 2 screen, 3 a full-area view, 4 SD card, 5 rhythm, 6 mixer, 7 autosave, 9 test page, 11 presets, 12 clipboards, 13 navigation, 14 MIDI out, 15 MIDI notes in, 16 MIDI transport, 17 sync). In System/Crash.log the line also has lr (loop passes since power-on) and sp (the audio load in tenths of a percent as last shown: near 1000 means the audio engine starved the main loop) P the page that was open (in hex, 18 is the DUB page) and L the audio load in percent (LOV from 99 %: the audio engine starved the loop). It is also added to System/Crash.log. Start-up is not watched.
↯
CRASH k AT address (V1.14): if the firmware crashes it now restarts by itself instead of freezing, and shows this until ADD or CLEAR. The details are added to System/Crash.log on the SD card: send that line to find the cause.
!
ERR nnnn instead of SLAVE on the Rhythm page counts MIDI bytes lost on the way in. It should stay at SLAVE.

Load and save screens (V1.18)

The LOAD, SAVE and CLEAR tabs of the FILE and DRUMKIT pages show all 100 slots at once instead of a bare number. The pictures are what the firmware draws (the same code, on a PC, with the device's fonts); the songs in them are examples.

▲▼
UP and DOWN move the cursor one slot (it wraps from 100 to 1). Hold the key and after about a second it moves ten slots at a time. LEFT and RIGHT change the tab (NEW, LOAD, SAVE, CLEAR) as before; ADD opens the confirmation and ADD again confirms, as before.
ⓘ
A slot shows what its file holds (no name is stored): tempo, MEASURE and BAR, the beats of the first bank, the instrument and sample names. The states come from the scan at start-up (so they are free); the card reads one file when the cursor stops. A file that is cut short (a save interrupted by an old firmware) shows as not readable.

Autosave

⏲
AUTOSAVE on the Metronome page (last field): OFF, 2, 5, 10 (default) or 20 minutes. When that time has passed and the song changed, it is written to the SD card: when stopped, or at a loop end while playing. With MIDI notes coming in it waits for STOP, so a note is never held back. It also waits while you turn knobs or press keys.
⏻
After a power cut or a crash: at power-on the Drumboy asks RESTORE AUTOSAVE?. ADD loads it, CLEAR throws it away. Then save it into a slot as usual.
⇄
LOAD and NEW autosave unsaved work first, so a wrong LOAD can be undone at the next power-on. SAVE into a slot removes the autosave files (the work is safe in the slot).
✓
Safe SAVE (V1.18): a song or drumkit is written to File_001.tmp, checked (written in full, read back and compared) and only then replaces the slot, so a failed or interrupted SAVE leaves the old file. After a power cut during a SAVE you may find File_001.tmp or File_001.new on the card: the next start deletes the unfinished one and finishes the checked one.
◌
SAVE on screen (V1.14): while an autosave writes, the status area shows SAVE; keys, the screen and incoming MIDI notes wait for that moment. SDERR in red means the write failed. The Metronome page shows under AUTOSAVE how many milliseconds the last one took.
✓
Two files (System/File/Autosave_A.rwf and _B) are written in turn and sealed with a checksum, so pulling the power in the middle of a write still leaves the previous copy. Your numbered slots are never touched.

nanoKONTROL Studio on the DUB desk (V1.20)

CC
Control changes, any channel. The Drumboy reads CC messages on any MIDI channel while MIDI IN is on, on every page, with the Studio's own numbers (no editor needed): faders 1 to 8 = CC 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 8, 9, 12, knobs 1 to 8 = CC 13 to 20, M buttons = CC 21 to 28, TRACK ◀ = CC 60 and TRACK ▶ = CC 61. The Studio reaches the Drumboy through the iPad's Loopy Pro and the Scarlett's MIDI out (or a USB host to DIN box). The full guide is docs/nanokontrol-studio.md.
▒
Eight strips, thirteen columns. The desk has ten layers, the ECHO and VERB returns and the LINE IN channel (fader = line-in volume, knob = its echo send, M = mute). TRACK ◀ and ▶ slide the Studio's eight strips along them, one column at a time; a cyan bar above the plates marks the eight columns it is on. Fader: a layer's LEVEL, the echo's WET, the reverb's WET. Knob: a layer's ECHO send, the echo's FEEDBACK, the reverb's DECAY. M button: a press mutes or unmutes the layer.
⇆
Take-over. After the strips slid, after a song loaded or at power-on, a fader does not match the screen. It takes hold only when it reaches the value shown (or crosses it), so a level never jumps; when the value is changed another way (the encoder, a loaded song, undo) it lets go again until it reaches the new value. A fader that sends hundreds of messages a second costs one change per pass: only the latest value is kept.
S R
Throws, X-Y pad and jog wheel (V1.21). S = an echo throw and R = a reverb throw on the strip: its send opens fully while the button is held (smoothly, about 8 ms to open and 54 ms to close; DUB mode). The X-Y pad: X is Filter 1's FREQ (the dub filter), Y the echo's FEEDBACK, and the top fifth of the pad goes past 100 %: the echo builds up and self-oscillates (its loop is soft-clipped only while boosted; pull the pad down to stop it; a new or loaded song clears it). The jog wheel steps the echo TIME (1/8 to 1/1). Their CC numbers (29 to 47) are a first guess: the DUB desk shows the last controller message in its bottom-left corner (CC 37 = 127) for 3 seconds, so the real ones can be read off the Drumboy.

Generators: noise and tones, and a hiss bed

A layer can play a generator instead of a sample (white, pink and brown noise, tape hiss, vinyl dust, a siren, a riser, a laser), and the mixer has a stereo hiss bed under the whole song. Everything runs at 44.1 kHz in 32-bit float, goes through the same router, pan, EQ, filters, sends and fades as a sample, and is saved in the song file.
